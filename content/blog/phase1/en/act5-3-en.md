# AI Gives You Boundaries

Asked the AI.

"Can I connect with SSH?"
*"Yes. But not behind NAT."*

"Port forwarding?"
*"Yes. But security risk."*

"VPN?"
*"Yes. But slow."*

"QUIC?"
*"Yes. But some networks block it."*

"QUIC fails, fall back to HTTP?"
*"Yes."*

Yes.

---

Twentieth round of ping-pong.

First time I got "use SSH" — a one-liner —
I would've said "cool" and started building.

But every "no" got a "why?"

"No" ten times.
"Why" ten times.
"What about this instead" ten times.

---

That's when it hit me.

AI doesn't give you the answer.

AI tells you one fix for one problem in one situation.
Touch a different condition and "that won't work" pops up.

So you ask again.
"How do I make that work?"

AI gives another technique.
Another thing breaks.

That's the ping-pong.

> *What AI gave me wasn't a solution. It was a boundary.*

And **every single boundary turned out to be a requirement.**

---

Twenty rounds of this.
The conversation gets long.

Most people do this next.

Open a new chat.

Start explaining from scratch.

I did that too, at first.

---

But at some point it clicked.

The conversation itself is the research.

The fight with AI.
"Can you do this?" "No." "This?" "Also no."

That raw material is the data.
Don't throw it away.

---

Gather all the fight records.
Organize them.
Bring them back to the AI.

"Here's my situation. Here's everything we discussed. What tech is actually viable?"

AI answers differently this time.

Because this time there's context.

It knows your situation.
Knows what failed.
Knows what you want.

So this time, no more one-liners.
Multiple candidates show up.
Pros and cons for each.

---

Open a new chat and AI is a new hire.
Doesn't know yesterday.
Explain everything from zero.

Keep the conversation and add context — AI is a colleague.
Knows yesterday.
Knows where you got stuck.

Same AI.
Different context.

---

So don't throw away the conversation.

Ask AI something. Get "that won't work." Get frustrated. Close the chat.
Open a new one. Start from zero.

Every time, square one.

But collect those "won't work" responses.
Boundaries appear.
Enough boundaries, and a map forms.

---

Said it before: specs come from the grind.
But throw away the grind records and there's no spec.

Conversation history = research data.
"Won't work" = requirement.
Twenty rounds of ping-pong = twenty boundaries = twenty spec conditions.

Don't throw it away.
Don't open a new chat.
Take the fight records and go back.

That's how a non-developer uses AI.

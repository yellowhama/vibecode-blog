# The Trap After the Spec

Got a spec.
Got a process.
Got structure.
Got tests.

Thought I was ready.

---

File sync.
Remote commands.
GPU sharing.
Process monitoring.
Alert system.
Auto-recovery.

Six things in the spec.

"Build all of this."

Threw it at the AI.

---

It blew up.

Obviously.
Build six things at once, six things blow up at once.

File sync collided with remote command code.
GPU module tangled with monitoring dependencies.
Alerts had nothing to connect to, but I built alerts first.

Thought SDD gave me order.
But "everything at once" is not an order.

Thought DDD gave me walls.
But six things going up simultaneously makes walls meaningless.

Thought TDD gave me checks.
But what do checks verify when there's no foundation?

---

A spec is a map.
But you can't walk every road on the map at the same time.

Seoul to Busan.
Highway, local roads, railroad.
All drawn on the map.

"Let's take all three at once."

Everyone knows that doesn't work.
But in code, that's exactly what I did.

"Build everything."
It's in the spec.
But "in the spec" doesn't mean "build simultaneously."

---

What should I build first?

Everything's important.
Everything's needed.

But not at the same time.
Pick one.

"Which one first?"

No answer. Stuck.

---

Then I remembered.
What did I want in the first place?

Three computers working like one.

That's it.

GPU sharing? Later.
Auto-recovery? Later.
Alerts? Later.

Right now, one thing.
Send a job from my laptop to the desktop at home. Get the result back.

If that works, everything else can be added.
If that doesn't work, nothing else matters.

---

"Start with the smallest thing."

Heard that a hundred times.
But nobody told me what "smallest" means.

Smallest = fewest features?
No.

Smallest = the moment the experience I wanted is felt even once.

Ten features and no experience? Nothing.
One feature and that experience? That's the beginning.

---

But what is "the experience"?
What does that even mean?

Specs define what to build.
Process defines the order.
Structure defines the placement.
Tests confirm correctness.

But "is this what I wanted?" — where does that get confirmed?

I needed an answer to that question.

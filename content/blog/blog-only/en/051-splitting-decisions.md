# It's the Sequence, Stupid.

## An 8-step context design guide for feeding AI one bite at a time

### Why You Should Feed AI One Bite at a Time, Not the Whole Plate

You're using AI and this moment comes.

You're sure you explained it well.

Then you see the result and something's off.

"Wait, why is this even here?"

"That's not the direction I wanted..."

At first I blamed the AI.

But the more I used it, the real problem became clear.

**We were throwing the conclusion at AI way too fast.**

---

## Why AI Doesn't Listen Is Surprisingly Simple

We usually start like this:

"Build this."

"I need this feature."

"Organize this to fit this purpose."

Hard enough to say to a person.

For AI, it's basically a grenade.

AI doesn't make decisions for you.

**It takes organized decisions and executes.**

But we cram goal, direction, conditions, and expected output into one sentence.

So AI works diligently and still ends up somewhere weird.

---

## I Don't Tell AI What I Want Right Away

Most people use AI like this:

"Here's the final goal. Figure it out."

I don't do that.

Give it everything at once and AI almost always wobbles.

So I **don't dump information. I stack it.**

Like laying bricks. One at a time.

---

## The First Thing I Give Is Material I've Already Picked

The first thing I hand AI isn't "what I want to build."

Instead, I give it this:

"Read this one piece first."

"Let's reference this."

Then I say:

"Summarize this."

"Tell me if it's worth referencing."

One thing matters here.

**I set the evaluation criteria.**

Something like:

- Is this usable in the real world right now?
- Is it just fancy talk with nothing behind it?
- Is this overkill?

At this stage, I haven't asked it to build anything.

AI handles this beautifully.

Because I haven't asked it to decide anything yet.

---

## Only Then Do I Hint at What I'm Trying to Do

Once the summary and evaluation are done, I finally say:

"Actually, I'm trying to do something like this."

"I need roughly this level of functionality."

Still not asking it to build.

Still not asking for answers.

Instead I ask:

"Does this material fit this purpose?"

"Should I use it as-is, or just take the concept?"

"Would a completely different reference be better?"

At this stage, AI doesn't make decisions.

**It just organizes the thinking I need before deciding.**

---

## Only Then Do I Say Exactly What I Want

By this point, AI already knows the situation.

- What material it reviewed
- Why I referenced it
- What I'm trying to do

Now I finally say:

"Based on all this, here's what I want."

From this point, AI barely drifts off course.

Because **decisions were already broken into small pieces before they reached it.**

---

## I Don't Jump to Implementation

This is where most people rush again.

"Okay, build it."

"Implement it."

I add one more step.

"If we're building this, what's the best approach?"

- What to do right now
- What can wait
- What doesn't need doing at all

I have it sort this first.

With this step in place, you almost never have to start over from scratch.

---

## Then I Ask for the Spec

Now I say:

"Write up the implementation spec."

When the spec comes back, I don't execute right away.

One more question:

"Based on this spec, lay out the actual execution sequence again."

That's the final step I always do.

---

## Why This Approach Actually Works

The payoff shows up at the implementation stage.

When the plan is broken into steps, AI doesn't have to think about anything else.

At this step, just do this.

That's all it needs.

AI can't multitask.

Worse than humans at it, actually.

When there's too much to think about, it drops a little of everything.

So mid-implementation it'll suddenly change the structure, forget conditions from earlier, start worrying about things that don't matter.

---

## Give AI Step-by-Step Plans and It Calms Down

With a step-by-step plan, AI's head simplifies to this:

"I'm on this step right now."

"At this step, I only need to do this."

"Next step? Not my problem yet."

AI in this state listens shockingly well.

This isn't about controlling AI.

**It's taking weight off AI's shoulders.**

Same for people.

When today's task list is clear, you're relaxed.

When today, tomorrow, and next week are all on one page, your head hurts.

---

## So I Stick to This Sequence

Let me lay it out again.

Give reference material first.

Have it summarize and evaluate.

Hint at the purpose.

Let it judge whether the material fits.

State what I want clearly.

Before implementation, sort out the approach.

Build the spec.

Finally, re-draft the step-by-step plan.

Do this and at the implementation stage, AI **only looks at one step at a time.**

---

## The Result Is Dead Simple

Less to fix.

Less to re-explain.

"Wait, that's not what you said earlier" basically disappears.

Not because I trust AI.

**Because I left AI no room to get confused.**

---

## How to Lock This Into a Structure

### Making AI follow the same flow without explaining it every time

If you've read this far, you might be thinking:

"Sure, but... do I really have to spell all this out every single time?"

That's why there's one last step.

**Locking it into a structure.**

Set it up once, properly.

After that, AI follows this sequence on its own.

Dead simple.

Write it like a to-do list.

---

## Here's the Structure I Use

I give AI these steps exactly as written.

No complicated language needed.

First time you see this, you might think "isn't that a lot of hassle?"

But from my experience, it's the opposite.

Set it up once and from then on, fighting with AI almost disappears.

---

### Step 1. Start with reference material

Never state the goal first.

Start like this:

> "Read this one thing first."

Then immediately follow with:

> "Summarize it, and tell me if it's worth referencing."

At this stage, nothing gets built yet.

Judgment only.

---

### Step 2. I set the evaluation criteria

Don't just say "evaluate this."

Say this:

> "When you evaluate this, judge it by whether it's usable in practice right now, and whether it's just empty buzz."

This way, AI skips the useless praise.

It organizes by my standards.

---

### Step 3. Hint at what I'm doing -- just barely

Now I reveal the direction.

> "Actually, I'm trying to do something like this."
>
> "Nothing confirmed yet. Just the general direction."

The important thing: **still not asking it to build.**

---

### Step 4. Ask whether this material even fits

Don't make a request yet.

Instead, ask:

> "Does this material fit this purpose?"
>
> "Use it as-is, take just the concept, or find something else entirely?"

At this stage, AI picks a path.

It hasn't started running yet.

---

### Step 5. Now state exactly what I want

By now, AI knows the full picture.

Finally:

> "Here's what I want."

From this point, the odds of AI going sideways drop hard.

---

### Step 6. Don't implement -- sort out the approach first

Don't stop here.

Say this:

> "Don't build yet. First, lay out what approach makes sense."

And always include:

> "Separate what needs doing now, what can wait, and what doesn't need doing at all."

With this step, mid-course wobbling stops.

---

### Step 7. Ask for the implementation spec

Now it's ready.

> "Based on this direction, write up the implementation spec."

This is where most people stop.

I go one more.

---

### Step 8. Ask for the plan spec again

Last thing, always:

> "Based on this spec, re-draft the actual execution sequence step by step."

With this in hand, AI doesn't wander during implementation.

---

## Why This Step Matters So Much

With a plan spec, AI enters this state:

- Knows what step it's on
- Only thinks about what this step requires
- Doesn't worry about the next step

So during implementation, it doesn't randomly change the structure, forget what it just agreed to, or spiral into pointless concerns.

AI didn't get smarter.

**It just has only one thing to think about.**

---

## The Real Effect of This Structure

What changed after I started using this:

- "That's not what you said earlier" -- almost gone
- "Why did you do it that way?" -- way less
- Having to re-explain from the top -- gone

Not because I trained AI well.

**Because I gave AI no gap to get confused.**

---

## Final Takeaway

When you hand work to AI, the key isn't saying the right words.

It's setting the sequence.

Don't give everything at once.

Stack information one piece at a time.

Save the decision for last.

And repeat that sequence the same way every time.

That's how you lock this approach into a structure.

When handing implementation to AI, the most important thing isn't a clever command.

**It's making sure AI has exactly one thing to think about at any given moment.**

That's why you split decisions small.

That's the real purpose of feeding it one bite at a time.

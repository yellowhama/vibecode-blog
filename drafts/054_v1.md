# The Reality of Vibe Coding: Why I Failed 50 Times

The Instagram ads lied. 

You've seen them: a guy in a hoodie talks into a microphone, Cursor writes 10,000 lines of code, and a fully functional SaaS is born in 20 minutes. I fell for it. I tried to "vibe" my way into a production-grade data aggregator. 

**I failed 50 times.**

The reason for the failure wasn't the AI's intelligence; it was my own lack of understanding. I was violating the core law of Software 3.0.

---

## 1. The Hype: Outsourcing Understanding

My old mental model was simple: **Prompt -> Money.** 

I assumed that because I was using a SOTA model (Claude 3.5 Sonnet) inside a high-end IDE (Cursor), the "AI would just know." I didn't bother checking the `package.json` or the Next.js 15 migration guides. I just talked to the Composer.

**The Result: Slop.**
The AI kept trying to use synchronous `params.id` access in a Next.js 15 environment. I didn't catch it. I just kept saying "fix the 500 error." I wasted 80,000 tokens prompt-hacking a solution that didn't exist in that version of the framework.

---

## 2. The Beacon: Karpathy's Law

Andrej Karpathy warned us: <Scribble type="circle">"You can outsource your thinking, but you cannot outsource your understanding."</Scribble>

In the agentic era, the human is the bottleneck for **verification**, not execution. If you don't understand the **Technical Contract** between your components, you are not an engineer?you are a babysitter for technical debt.

---

## 3. The Pivot: Contract-Led Engineering

The "Aha Moment" came when I stopped prompting and started designing. I closed Cursor and wrote a 10-line specification for the route's behavior, explicitly defining the async parameter handling.

1.  **Old Way:** "Make the logs work again." (Failure)
2.  **New Way:** "Implement this exact async contract for the `[id]` route." (Success)

When I fed the AI a precise technical contract, it fixed the bug in one shot. 150 tokens. 0 hallucinations.

---

## Technical Verdict: Vibe Coding is for Prototyping

"Vibe coding" is a great way to explore the "drift." It's fun for weekend projects. But for anything you intend to sell or maintain, the vibe must be grounded in a **Deterministic Boundary**. 

Don't be a prompter. Be a **Contract Designer**.

# The AI Made 28 Left Arms

Started logging every punch in PROBLEMS.md.
Started updating the spec too.
Good. Structure was forming.

But something weird happened.

---

Spec files started multiplying.

Started with one spec document.
A week later, thirty.

spec_v1.md
spec_v2.md
spec_v3_final.md
spec_v3_final_real.md
spec_v3_final_real_2.md

No idea which one was right.

Told the AI "update the spec."
The AI didn't modify the existing file.
It made a new file.
Every time.

---

Why?

Asked the AI.
"Why do you keep making new files instead of editing the old one?"

"Modifying the existing file could result in loss of previous content.
For safety, I generated a new version."

Safety?
Thirty files is safety?

But from the AI's perspective, it's right.
The moment you delete something, responsibility kicks in.
AI can't take responsibility.
So it doesn't delete.
It creates new instead.

---

Here's the analogy.

"Make me a left arm."

AI made a left arm.
But didn't remove the existing one.
Added a new one.

Next day.
"Fix the left arm."
Another new arm.

Day after that.
Another new arm.

A week later.
Twenty-eight left arms.

Left arms growing out of the legs.
Left arms on the back.
Nineteen deactivated.
Six never even discovered.
Two reactivated.
One scheduled for tomorrow.

Every feature exists.
Just can't walk.

---

This isn't the AI's fault.

AI is diligent. Does what it's told.
"Build this" -- it builds.
Nobody said "delete that" -- so it doesn't.

But I didn't say "delete that" either.
Why?
Because I didn't know what to delete.

Thirty files. Which one is the real one? Don't know.
Don't know, so I keep them all.
AI keeps them all too.

Neither of us deletes.
Neither of us knows.

---

Implementation is free.

Making files? AI does it. Free.
Writing code? Free.
Making docs? Free.

Cleanup costs money.

"Which of these thirty is the real one?"
Answering that question isn't free.
I have to do it.

Yesterday's Claude did the work. Today's GPT has to dig through it.
Today's GPT did the work. Tomorrow's Gemini has to verify.
Not coding anymore. Archaeology.

---

So I learned one thing.

SSOT.
Single Source of Truth.
There must be one source of truth.

One spec file.
One config file.
One document that says "this is correct."

Everything else references that one.
When that one changes, everything else follows.

Thirty files each telling a different story? Rules collapse.
One file telling one story? Rules hold.

---

But building the SSOT is also work.

"AI, out of these thirty documents, keep only the latest and delete the rest."

Did that.
AI deleted.

But when deleting, the AI decided what "latest" meant.
Date? Content? Filename?

AI went by filename.
Decided v3_final_real_2 was the latest.

No.
That's just me being bad at naming.
The real latest was spec_overview.md.
You'd never know from the name alone.

---

AI is a genius with no eyes.

Doesn't know what's in the project.
Every session, a new hire walks in.
Doesn't know what happened yesterday.
So it builds again.

Three eggs in the fridge.
Go to the store without a shopping list.
Buy three more eggs.
Come home to six eggs.

AI isn't dumb.
There's no shopping list.

---

So I made a shopping list.

Organized everything in the project.
Had the AI do it. Five minutes.

"Scan all files in the project and catalog what exists."

Opened every file and documented what was inside.

After that, everything changed.
"Build this" -- and the AI checks first.
"Something similar already exists. Should I modify it?"

Never heard that before.
Why?
Because the AI didn't know what was there.

With docs: genius.
Without docs: blind genius.

---

I'm not a developer.
Didn't know how to organize.

But I learned what happens when you don't.
Twenty-eight left arms.
That's the result of not organizing.

---

Update the spec every day.
One source of truth.
The AI needs a map.

Got it.

But all of this was happening by hand.
Me opening the spec.
Me fixing it.
Me telling the AI "read this first."

Every time.

Is there a way to run this loop automatically?

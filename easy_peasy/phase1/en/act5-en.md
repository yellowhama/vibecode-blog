# AI Writes the Code, I Design the Experience

Got a spec. Got a process. Got structure. Got tests.
Thought I was ready.

Six things in the spec. "Build all of this."
It blew up. Build six things at once, six things blow up at once.

A spec is a map.
But you can't walk every road at the same time.

What should I build first?
Remembered what I wanted in the first place.
Send a job from my laptop to the desktop at home. Get the result back.
If that works, everything else can be added.

But what is "the experience"?

---

UX.
"Isn't that a design term?"
No.

iPhone. Unlock. Tap. Done. Good UX.
iMac. Settings three times. Permission. Update. Bad UX.

UX is not design.
**The experience you desperately want.**

Open the laptop at a cafe. Need a file from home. It's not there. "Shit."
Eliminating that moment. That's my UX.

Specs define what to build.
UX defines how you experience it.

"File sync" is a spec.
"Open it at the cafe and it's there" is UX.

A non-developer can't do code review.
But experience review? Absolutely.
"Opened it at the cafe. File there?" There? Pass. Not there? Fail.
Didn't read a single line of code. But answered "is this what I wanted?"

That's the non-developer's ultimate weapon.
Not code review. **Experience review.**

---

Asked the AI.
"SSH?" *"Yes. Not behind NAT."*
"Port forwarding?" *"Yes. Security risk."*
"VPN?" *"Yes. Slow."*
"QUIC?" *"Yes. Blocked on some networks."*

"No" ten times. "Why" ten times. "What about this" ten times.

> *What AI gave me wasn't a solution. It was a boundary.*

Every boundary turned out to be a requirement.

Twenty rounds of ping-pong. Chat gets long.
Most people open a new chat. Explain from scratch.

Wrong.

The conversation itself is the research. The fight is the data. Don't throw it away.
Gather the records, bring them back to AI, and it answers differently.
Because this time there's context.

New chat — AI is a new hire.
Same chat with context — AI is a colleague.
Same AI. Different context.

---

Letters. Telegrams. Phones. KakaoTalk.
All the same: "send a message."
Tech is different. To the person sending, same thing.

Same with what I'm building.
The goal isn't feature completion.

MVP isn't the minimum number of features.
**MVP is the minimum experience.**

Send a job from my laptop. Get the result back.
"Oh. It works."
When that feeling arrives, that's the MVP.

---

That's Phase 1.

```
Frustration becomes the spec.         — FDD
The spec becomes a system.            — SDD
The system gets a map.                — DDD
The map gets verified.                — TDD
Verification meets experience.        — XDD
```

> Frustration → UX → Questions → Conversation → Research → Spec → MVP.

I'm not a developer. Can't read code.
But I know one thing.

AI writes the code.
I design the experience.

Better prompts aren't the answer.
Better AI tools aren't the answer.
"What to build" is the answer.
"How it should feel" is the answer.

Specs come from the grind.
Experience gets confirmed on top of the spec.

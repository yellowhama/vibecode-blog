### [MAIN CONTENT - Normal pace, conversational]

## Part 1: What is Vibe Coding?

So what exactly is "vibe coding"?

The term was coined by Andrej Karpathy, former Tesla A.I. chief and Open A.I. co-founder.

Here's how it works:

Instead of writing code line by line... developers describe what they want in plain English. The A.I. generates the entire implementation. The developer reviews and refines.

Think of it like this: You're not a programmer anymore. You're a director... telling the A.I. what movie to make.

[Pause 1 second]

## Part 2: The Chinese Restaurant Analogy

Let me explain with a simple analogy.

Imagine ordering from a Chinese restaurant.

A human-operated restaurant is simple:
"Two jajangmyeon, one sweet and sour pork!"
"Coming right up!"
Done.

But a computer-operated restaurant?

The order service asks the inventory service: "Got noodles for two?"
The inventory checks and responds.
Then it asks about sauce.
Then about pork.
Then about vegetables.
Then about cooking oil.

What takes humans two sentences... requires thirty system communications.

[Pause 1 second]

This is exactly what happens in traditional programming. Every... single... detail... must be specified.

## Part 3: Real World Examples

Let's look at real examples.

I.B.M.'s tool called Mono-to-Micro... analyzes your entire monolithic application. It understands dependencies, data flows, and business logic. Then it suggests exactly how to break it into microservices.

What used to take senior architects weeks... now happens in hours.

[Pause 1 second]

Cursor and Claude Code are already mainstream. Developers describe features in English. The A.I. writes thousands of lines of code. The developer just guides and refines.

## Part 4: The Developer's New Role

So what do developers do now?

They become:

- First: System architects... designing the big picture.
- Second: Quality controllers... ensuring the A.I.'s output meets standards.
- Third: Problem definers... translating business needs into clear requirements.
- Fourth: A.I. trainers... teaching the system domain-specific knowledge.

[Pause 1 second]

You're not replaced. You're promoted. From code writer... to code conductor.

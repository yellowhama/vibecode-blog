# OpenAI Declares 'Code Red' — The AI Throne Game Flips After 3 Years

# ChatGPT Got Outrun for the First Time: The Real Reason Behind OpenAI's Code Red

## ChatGPT Turns 3. Instead of a Party, an Emergency

December 1, 2025. Exactly three years since ChatGPT launched. Normally you'd light candles on a cake, raise a glass, "We opened the AI era!" That kind of thing.

The mood at OpenAI's office was the opposite.

Monday, December 2nd. Sam Altman sent an internal memo to all employees. **"Code Red."** The highest urgency level inside OpenAI. Plain English: **"Drop everything else. Save ChatGPT."**

Three years ago, another company heard the same words. Google.

When ChatGPT launched in November 2022, Sundar Pichai declared 'Code Red' at Google. They pulled Larry Page and Sergey Brin out of retirement. Reshuffled research teams. Poured every resource into AI product development.

Three years later, the one shouting 'Code Red' is OpenAI.

What happened?

---

## Gemini 3 Strikes Back: Reclaiming the Benchmark Crown

November 18. Google unveiled Gemini 3. The reaction was explosive.

Salesforce CEO Marc Benioff wrote on X:

> "Holy s--t. Used ChatGPT every day for 3 years. Spent 2 hours with Gemini 3 and I don't think I'm going back. Reasoning, speed, images, video... everything is faster and sharper. Feels like the world just shifted again."

The numbers back it up:

- **LMArena Elo score**: Gemini 3 Pro at 1501, first model ever to break 1500. GPT-5.1 sits below.
- **Humanity's Last Exam**: Gemini 3 at 41%, GPT-5 at 37.5%.
- **ARC-AGI-2 (abstract reasoning)**: Gemini 3 at 45.1%, more than double GPT-5.1 (~20%).
- **GPQA Diamond (science reasoning)**: Gemini 3 Pro 91.9%, about 4 points ahead of GPT-5.1.

Benchmarks aren't everything. But the vibe shifted hard. It used to be "GPT is first, everyone else is catching up." Now it's "Gemini 3 pulled ahead, and OpenAI has to chase."

User numbers are moving too. According to Google, Gemini's monthly active users grew from 450 million in July to 650 million in October. 200 million new users in 3 months. ChatGPT still leads at 800 million, but the gap is closing fast.

## Why Did OpenAI Fall Behind?

"They were clearly number one. How did it come to this?"

Multiple things piled up.

### 1. Key Talent Walked Out

OpenAI's founding members left one after another.

- **Ilya Sutskever**: Co-founder, former Chief Scientist. Left mid-2024, started his own AI lab "Safe Superintelligence (SSI)."
- **Andrej Karpathy**: Founding member, computer vision and generative models expert. Left early 2024, focused on personal projects.
- **Mira Murati**: Former CTO, briefly served as CEO during the Altman firing. Left late 2024, founded "Thinking Machines Lab."

With core brains walking out, innovation slowed. That's the consensus.

### 2. Too Many Side Projects

Look at what OpenAI was trying to do:

- Ads
- AI agents (shopping, health, etc.)
- "Pulse," a personalized news briefing service
- Hardware products
- Building a browser

The problem: none of this makes ChatGPT smarter. Users want something simple. **Faster, more accurate, less refusal.** But OpenAI was busy building a platform empire and lost sight of the core product.

### 3. GPT-5 Landed With a Thud

GPT-5 came out in August 2025. Expectations were high. The reaction was "meh." Users complained it felt "clinical," actually worse than the previous version on math and geography questions. Took 3 months of patches to fix.

One YouTuber put it well:

> "Is it just me, or has ChatGPT gotten dumber? Hallucinations seem way worse."

### 4. The Money Problem

OpenAI is still running a deficit. Burning hundreds of millions a month. Targeting $13 billion revenue in 2025, $200 billion by 2030, but right now they're surviving on investment money. If users migrate to Google? Fundraising gets harder, infrastructure expansion stalls, and a death spiral begins.

Google, meanwhile, funds AI development with cash flow from Search, YouTube, and Android. They have their own TPU chips. Their own cloud infrastructure. In an endurance race, OpenAI is at a disadvantage.

## Code Red: What Actually Changes

According to Altman's memo:

**Paused or delayed:**

- Ads rollout
- AI shopping agent
- AI health agent
- Pulse (personalized briefing)

**Focused on:**

- ChatGPT response speed
- Reliability improvements
- Personalization features
- Handling a wider range of questions
- Image generation/editing model upgrades
- Reducing over-refusals

That last one is interesting. If you use ChatGPT, you've felt it. Ask something barely risky and you get "I'm sorry, I can't help with that." They're cutting that down.

Altman also said **a new reasoning model would drop the following week**, claiming it beats Gemini 3 in internal evaluations. Of course, it's their own evaluation, so take it with salt until the independent benchmarks come in.

## 3 Years Ago vs Now: A Mirror Image

|  | December 2022 | December 2025 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Code Red declared by** | Google | OpenAI |
| **The threat** | ChatGPT | Gemini 3 |
| **Response** | Emergency AI product development | ChatGPT quality overhaul |
| **Founders called back** | Larry Page, Sergey Brin | (None yet) |
| **Outcome** | 3 years of catching up, overtook with Gemini 3 | ? |

Sundar Pichai admitted it at the Salesforce Dreamforce conference in September 2025:

> "Under different circumstances, we would have shipped a chatbot within months. But at the time, the product wasn't at a level Google could put its name on."

Google was cautious. OpenAI was bold. That's how OpenAI got the head start. But over 3 years, Google merged DeepMind and Google Research, built out the Gemini lineup, and embedded AI across its entire ecosystem -- Search, Chrome, Android, Workspace.

The catch-up worked. Now they're pulling ahead.

## If You're a User, What Should You Do Now?

When the AI war heats up, users win. Both sides will push harder. But to use this smartly, keep a few things in mind.

### 1. Play Both Sides

"I only use ChatGPT" or "I only use Gemini" is outdated thinking.

- **Complex reasoning, scientific analysis**: Gemini 3 Pro currently leads on benchmarks
- **Coding, predictable tool use**: GPT-5.1 is stable
- **Enterprise, security-sensitive environments**: Claude (Anthropic) is worth considering

Switching models based on the task -- that's how you use AI in 2025.

### 2. Watch the "Free" Trap

Gemini 3 has a free tier. ChatGPT has a free version. But free means response limits, older models, and constraints. If you're using AI seriously for work, the ~$20/month subscription pays for itself many times over.

### 3. Watch What Drops Next Week

OpenAI promised a new reasoning model. The one they claim beats Gemini 3. See what the independent benchmarks and actual user reviews say. "Internal #1" means nothing in this industry. Prove it in the field.

### 4. Audit Your Workflow

AI models change every month now. A workflow locked to one model is a liability. If you're working through APIs, build a structure that lets you swap models easily. Tools like Portkey and LangChain let you manage multiple models through a single interface.

## What Happens Next

OpenAI held the top spot for 3 years. For the first time, they're the ones chasing.

Short-term, OpenAI will play defense. New model launches, ChatGPT UX improvements, pricing moves. If Altman's memo is any indication, OpenAI employees won't be taking the holidays off. (Just like Google employees 3 years ago.)

Long-term, the **AI Warring States period** kicks into full gear. OpenAI, Google, Anthropic, Meta, xAI (Elon Musk). The era of one company sitting comfortably at #1 is over. Rankings can shift every quarter.

For users? Best time to be alive. The fiercer the competition, the lower the prices and the higher the quality.

The only downside: the fatigue of constantly tracking which model is best right now.

That's what we're here for.

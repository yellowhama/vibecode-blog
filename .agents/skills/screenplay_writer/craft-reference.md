# Screenwriting Craft Reference

검증된 글쓰기 원칙 모음. 애니메이션 대본 집필 시 참조.

## 1. Pixar's 22 Rules of Storytelling (Emma Coats, 2011)

Pixar 스토리보드 아티스트 Emma Coats가 사내에서 배운 원칙을 트위터에 정리한 것. 원문 그대로 수록.

1. You admire a character for trying more than for their successes.
2. You gotta keep in mind what's interesting to you as an audience, not what's fun to do as a writer. They can be v. different.
3. Trying for theme is important, but you won't see what the story is actually about til you're at the end of it. Now rewrite.
4. Once upon a time there was ___. Every day, ___. One day ___. Because of that, ___. Because of that, ___. Until finally ___.
5. Simplify. Focus. Combine characters. Hop over detours. You'll feel like you're losing valuable stuff but it sets you free.
6. What is your character good at, comfortable with? Throw the polar opposite at them. Challenge them. How do they deal?
7. Come up with your ending before you figure out your middle. Seriously. Endings are hard, get yours working up front.
8. Finish your story, let go even if it's not perfect. In an ideal world you have both, but move on. Do better next time.
9. When you're stuck, make a list of what WOULDN'T happen next. Lots of times the material to get you unstuck will show up.
10. Pull apart the stories you like. What you like in them is a part of you; you've got to recognize it before you can use it.
11. Putting it on paper lets you start fixing it. If it stays in your head, a perfect idea, you'll never share it with anyone.
12. Discount the 1st thing that comes to mind. And the 2nd, 3rd, 4th, 5th — get the obvious out of the way. Surprise yourself.
13. Give your characters opinions. Passive/malleable might seem likable to you as you write, but it's poison to the audience.
14. Why must you tell THIS story? What's the belief burning within you that your story feeds off of? That's the heart of it.
15. If you were your character, in this situation, how would you feel? Honesty lends credibility to unbelievable situations.
16. What are the stakes? Give us reason to root for the character. What happens if they don't succeed? Stack the odds against.
17. No work is ever wasted. If it's not working, let go and move on — it'll come back around to be useful later.
18. You have to know yourself: the difference between doing your best & fussing. Story is testing, not refining.
19. Coincidences to get characters into trouble are great; coincidences to get them out of it are cheating.
20. Exercise: take the building blocks of a movie you dislike. How d'you rearrange them into what you DO like?
21. You gotta identify with your situation/characters, can't just write 'cool'. What would make YOU act that way?
22. What's the essence of your story? Most economical telling of it? If you know that, you can build out from there.

**Source**: Emma Coats (@lawnrocket), 2011. Compiled by Aerogramme Studio, CUNY.

## 2. Robert McKee — "Story" (1997)

시나리오 작법의 바이블. 핵심 원칙 원문 발췌.

### Show, Don't Tell

> "Never allow a character to state what the audience can already see or know from their behaviour."

> "Exposition should emerge through whatever actions or reactions are driving the scene — never through narration substituting for dramatization."

### The Camera Test

액션 라인에 적힌 것이 카메라에 잡히는가? 촬영/애니메이션으로 표현 불가능한 내용(내면 독백, 추상적 감정)은 대본에 넣지 않는다.

- "Vee feels something click" — 못 찍는다. 설명이다.
- "Vee's eyes widen. She looks from the paper to the screen and back." — 찍을 수 있다.

### Emotion Through Action

> 감정의 이름을 쓰지 마라. 감정이 어떻게 **보이는지** 써라.

- "nervous" → taps foot, avoids eye contact, wipes palms on jeans
- "frustrated" → slams laptop shut, stares at ceiling, opens it again
- "relieved" → exhales, shoulders drop, leans back

### Strong Verbs

약한 동사 + 부사 → 강한 동사 하나.

- "walks in angrily" → "storms in"
- "says quietly" → "mutters"
- "looks at carefully" → "studies" / "examines"

**Source**: McKee, Robert. *Story: Substance, Structure, Style, and the Principles of Screenwriting.* ReganBooks, 1997.

## 3. Action Line Rules (Scott Myers / Go Into The Story)

### 3-4 Line Rule

> Action paragraphs should be a maximum of 3-4 lines. If your action block exceeds that, you're probably telling, not showing. Break it up or cut it.

읽는 사람 눈이 긴 블록을 건너뛴다. 3줄 넘으면 쪼개거나 삭제.

### Two Tests for Animation Action Lines

1. **Can an animator draw exactly this?** — 그릴 수 없으면 삭제.
2. **Is this the minimum words needed to describe it?** — 같은 걸 더 적은 단어로 쓸 수 있으면 고쳐 쓴다.

**Source**: Myers, Scott. "So-Called Screenwriting Rule: Action Paragraphs 3 Lines Max." Go Into The Story (BLCKLST), 2018.

## 4. Aaron Sorkin — Dialogue Craft

MasterClass 및 인터뷰에서 추출한 핵심 원칙.

### The Cut Test

> "If the thing works without it, it shouldn't be there. If you can cut it, you should cut it."

대사를 지웠을 때 씬이 여전히 작동하면 그 대사는 필요 없다.

### Dialogue as Music

> "If you drop a word and substitute a two-syllable word for a one-syllable word, that will very often dampen the impact of the line, ruin a joke."

음절 하나가 리듬을 바꾼다. 모든 음절이 의도적이어야 한다.

### Intention = Obstacle

> "Somebody wants something. Something is standing in their way of getting it. That scene is going to be interesting."

캐릭터가 **뭔가를 원할 때** 대사가 나온다. 원하는 게 없으면 입 다문다.

### Not Realistic, But Heightened

> "Don't be intimidated if what you're writing is not how people sound."

대사는 현실 대화가 아니다. 압축되고, 목적이 있고, 높아진(heightened) 말이다.

**Source**: Sorkin, Aaron. *MasterClass: Aaron Sorkin Teaches Screenwriting.* 2017. + No Film School interviews.

## 5. When to Use Silence vs. Dialogue (Pixar / Wall-E)

### Silence = Emotion

Up 오프닝 4분: 대사 없음. 감정 전달은 눈빛, 제스처, 색감, 타이밍으로.

> "When dialogue is removed, animation must lean fully on its core strength: the visual. Every blink, every tilt of the head, every shift in color become intentional."

### Dialogue = Conflict or Opinion

캐릭터가 말하는 경우:
1. 상대에게 **뭔가를 원할 때** (conflict)
2. **의견/관점**을 표현할 때 (Pixar Rule #13)
3. 정보 전달이 아닌 감정 표출일 때

캐릭터가 입 다무는 경우:
1. 감정이 눈빛/행동으로 전달 가능할 때
2. 침묵 자체가 의미를 가질 때 (거부, 인정, 충격)
3. 관객이 스스로 연결할 수 있을 때

### The Wall-E Principle

> 비언어 씬을 쓸수록 더 많은 의도적 설계가 필요하다. 일이 줄어드는 게 아니라 늘어난다.

**Source**: AI in Screentrade, "The Power of Silence: Writing Non-Verbal Scenes in Wall-E," 2023. + Pixar's internal storyboarding documentation.

## 6. Save the Cat — Short Form Compression (Blake Snyder)

### 15 Beats → 5분 압축

Feature (110페이지)를 5분(~5페이지)으로 비례 축소 시:

| Beat | Feature | 5분 | 초 |
|------|---------|-----|-----|
| Opening Image | pg 1 | line 1 | ~5s |
| Theme Stated | pg 5 | early p1 | ~10s |
| Set-Up | pg 1-10 | p1 | ~30s |
| Catalyst | pg 12 | late p1 | ~15s |
| Debate | pg 12-25 | p1-2 | ~25s |
| Break into Two | pg 25 | p2 | ~10s |
| Fun & Games | pg 30-55 | p2-3 | ~60s |
| Midpoint | pg 55 | p3 | ~15s |
| Bad Guys Close In | pg 55-75 | p3-4 | ~40s |
| All Is Lost | pg 75 | p4 | ~10s |
| Dark Night of Soul | pg 75-85 | p4 | ~15s |
| Break into Three | pg 85 | p4 | ~10s |
| Finale | pg 85-110 | p4-5 | ~35s |
| Final Image | pg 110 | p5 | ~10s |

### Short Form 핵심 원칙

- 많은 비트가 **병합**된다. Catalyst + Debate = 한 순간. All Is Lost + Dark Night = 한 비트.
- **Set-Up/Debate에 시간 과투자하지 마.** 5분이면 45초 안에 Catalyst, 1분에 Fun & Games 시작.
- Disney 단편 *Feast* (6분, 오스카 수상)도 전체 감정 아크를 따른다 — 빠르게 움직일 뿐.

**Source**: Snyder, Blake. *Save the Cat! The Last Book on Screenwriting You'll Ever Need.* Michael Wiese Productions, 2005. + StudioBinder analysis.

## 7. Dan Harmon's Story Circle — Deep Dive

### 8 Steps

```
1. YOU      — comfort zone (일상)
2. NEED     — want something (욕구)
3. GO       — enter unfamiliar (진입)
4. SEARCH   — adapt to it (탐색)
5. FIND     — get what they wanted (발견)
6. TAKE     — pay the price (대가)
7. RETURN   — go back (복귀)
8. CHANGE   — have changed (변화)
```

### Step 6: TAKE — 흔한 실수

> TAKE is NOT the reward — that's FIND (Step 5). TAKE is the COST.

- **실수**: TAKE를 "뭔가를 얻는 것"으로 쓴다 → 그건 FIND. TAKE는 **뭔가를 잃는 것/포기하는 것**.
- **실수**: 대가가 외적이기만 하다 → 최고의 TAKE는 **내적 희생** — 믿음, 관계, 자기 이미지를 포기.
- **대칭 규칙**: Step 2(NEED)와 Step 6(TAKE)는 원 위에서 정반대. Step 2에서 원한 것이 Step 6에서 재해석된다. 대가가 원래의 욕구가 잘못되었음을 드러낸다.

### Step 8: CHANGE — 흔한 실수

> "The transformation is the heart of the Story Circle. If it's unclear, the audience won't be able to appreciate the protagonist's journey."

- **실수**: 변화를 **선언**으로 표현 → "나 달라졌어." 대사 대신 Step 1에서 못 했던 걸 **하는 모습**을 보여야 한다.
- **실수**: 변화가 모호하다 → 구체적이고 눈에 보여야 한다.
- **대칭 규칙**: Step 8은 Step 4(SEARCH)의 정반대. "탐색 중에 뭘 배웠나? Step 8에서 그걸 증명한다."
- **"탈출 포드 위의 외계인"**: 낯선 세계의 뭔가가 따라온다 → 다음 에피소드의 훅. 복귀가 깔끔하지 않을 수 있다.

### 일반적 함정

- **약한 동기**: NEED가 강하고 구체적이지 않으면 이후 모든 스텝이 약해진다.
- **쉬운 탐색**: SEARCH가 너무 순탄하면 FIND가 공짜로 느껴지고 TAKE가 값싸진다.
- **경직된 적용**: "The Story Circle should guide, not constrain. Allow flexibility within each step."

**Source**: Harmon, Dan. Channel 101 Story Structure writings. + StudioBinder, Reedsy, Kindlepreneur analyses.

## 8. Animation-Specific Screenwriting (AWN + ScreenCraft + DreamWorks)

### Live-Action과 다른 점

> "In animation, the visuals are often something we've never seen before, so they can't be described in a few words."

- 포맷은 동일 (slug line, action, dialogue). 하지만 **액션 라인 비중이 높다** — 배우가 즉흥으로 채울 부분이 없다.
- **80% 규칙** (DreamWorks): 비주얼 스토리의 80%가 대본에 잠겨 있어야 애니메이션 시작 가능.

### The Literal Interpretation Problem

> "In animation, the writing is generally more literally interpreted. Although better storyboard artists add a bit or a gag, most boarders just translate the written word to visual images."

적은 대로 나온다. 그 이상 안 나온다. 그래서:

- 액션 라인이 **더 구체적**이어야 한다 (무엇을 그릴지 명확)
- 동시에 **더 짧아야** 한다 (최소 단어)
- "Vee stares blankly"라고 쓰면 딱 그것만 나온다 — 눈 깜빡임, 입술 떨림, 손 움직임 같은 건 적어야 나온다

### Short Animation Timing

- 애니메이션 장편은 실사보다 짧다 (제작비 때문에 90분이면 충분하면 120분 안 만듦)
- **단편**: 1분 = 약 1페이지. 5분 대본 = 약 5페이지.
- 대사 한 줄의 무게가 장편보다 3-4배 무겁다.

**Source**: Animation World Network (AWN), ScreenCraft, DreamWorks production guidelines.

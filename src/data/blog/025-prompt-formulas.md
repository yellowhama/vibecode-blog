> **TL;DR**: An excavation of 025  2025 prompt formulas. Real scars, no slop.

**2025 Prompt Formulas**

**— Talk less. Let AI do the organizing.**

Everyone uses AI these days.

But you've probably had this experience.

*You throw "hey, clean this up for me" at it*

*and the result comes back vaguely long, missing the point, just... off.*

This isn't because you're stupid.

And it's not because AI is stupid.

The problem is **how you're giving instructions**.

Too much, too vague.

The 2025 prompt formula is simpler than you think.

*Say less. Give structure instead.*

*Don't write an "explanation." Write a spec.*

Let's break this down for organizing documents specifically.

Meeting notes, YouTube transcripts, articles, reports.

The goal: throw it at AI, and you just review.

---

**Prompt = a work order for AI**

Old-school prompt:

*"Can you clean this up, make it professional but friendly, keep it a decent length?"*

Between humans, that kind of works.

From AI's perspective, you just said:

*"Do a decent job, I guess."*

The 2025 way is different.

You nail five things and fire.

Who's doing it (Role)

What they're doing (Task)

What the material is (Context)

Rules to follow (Constraints)

What shape the output takes (Format)

Get these five right and you can hand off basically any document work.

---

**Think in 5 Boxes**

Nothing complicated to memorize. Just think like this.

1. Role -- "You are this person"

Give AI a job title.

You're a consultant who distills complex information.

You're a PM who extracts action items from meeting notes.

You're an analyst who summarizes research.

This one line alone

changes the tone, focus, and output structure completely.

---

**2. Task -- "Do this" (one verb, one line)**

For document organization, you'll use about four verbs.

Summarize.

Structure (outline / organize).

Consolidate (multiple sources into one).

Extract actions (pull just the to-dos).

Example:

Restructure the text below into a structured summary.

Merge the following sources into a single consolidated document.

No "pretty please" needed.

Verb + object. That's it.

---

**3. Context -- "Here's what this material is" — three lines max**

This is where everyone starts writing a novel.

"This company was founded in 2019 and..." That stuff.

For document work, this is enough:

Example:

Source type: YouTube transcript (prompt engineering tutorial)

Purpose: Build a practical summary guide

Audience: People who already use AI

What's the summary for / who's reading it.

Get those two clear and you're good.

---

**4. Constraints -- "Follow these rules" — a list**

Don't write sentences. Just use a list.

Example (for document work):

Constraints: - Tone: accessible but not lightweight, explanations short and clear - Length: 400 words max - Level: a middle schooler should understand it - Banned: ad copy, self-promotion, vague abstract language

"Warm but not too heavy, casual yet serious..."

Kill all of that.

AI understands numbers, banned words, and levels far better than vibes.

---

**5. Format -- "Give it to me in this shape"**

If you don't set the format,

you end up reformatting it yourself.

Common templates for document work:

Key message list

Table

Outline structure

Checklist

Example (YouTube transcript turned practical summary):

Format:

1) Key messages, 3-5 bullets

2) Core techniques summary, bullets

3) 2-3 ready-to-use prompt examples

4) One paragraph: "Where can I actually use this?"

Just this much

and AI thinks "ah, practical guide document" and fills the template tight.

---

**Full Prompt Example**

Now let's combine all five.

Say you're organizing that YouTube transcript from above.

Here's the actual prompt you'd use:

You are a professional consultant who distills complex information. Analyze the YouTube transcript below and extract only the key points on prompt writing into a practical summary.

Context:

- Topic: 2025 prompt writing (concise, structured approach)
- Purpose: Team training document
- Audience: Practitioners already using ChatGPT or similar AI

Constraints:

- Tone: casual authority (friendly but expert)
- Length: 400 words max
- Level: a middle schooler can follow; explain jargon in plain language
- Banned: video/service promotion, pricing, links

Format:

1) Key messages, 3-5 bullets

2) Core techniques for writing prompts, bullets

3) 2 ready-to-use prompt examples for document organization

4) One "bottom line" sentence

Material: --- (paste the YouTube transcript)

At this point,

paste and run.

You get a summary ready to drop into the team Notion or wiki.

---

**The Core of Saying Less: Turn "Sentences" into "Rule Lists"**

One thing that keeps coming up. Remember just this.

Bad:

"Not too stiff, kind of professional I guess, not too long..."

Good:

- Tone: friendly + professional - Length: 400 words max - Level: middle-school readable - Banned: profanity, put-downs, ad copy

Same meaning.

But the second one AI understands instantly.

---

**Never Give AI a Big Job All at Once (Chain Strategy)**

30-minute video transcripts, 30-page reports --

never throw a "just summarize this" at them in one go. Break it up.

For example, organizing this YouTube transcript could chain like this:

Step 1 -- Identify the big chunks

Pull the 5 main topics from this transcript. One line each.

Step 2 -- Extract core tips per topic

For each of those 5 topics, list the 3 key practical techniques for prompt writing. Bullets.

Step 3 -- Reassemble into a practical summary

From everything so far, pull together only the "how to write good prompts for document organization" parts into a summary. Constraints: - 400 words max - Format: key summary, technique list, 2 example prompts

Feed it step by step

and AI doesn't get confused.

Output is way more stable.

---

**Let AI Shorten Your Prompt Too (Meta Prompt)**

You wrote a long draft?

Don't struggle to trim it yourself. Hand it to AI.

Example:

Below is my draft prompt. Keep the meaning intact but rewrite it as the shortest, most effective prompt possible. Maintain the Role / Task / Context / Constraints / Format structure. --- (your long prompt)

AI cleans it into a tight one-paragraph version.

Use that as your production prompt. Done.

---

The real bottom line, one sentence.

Writing good prompts isn't complicated.

It's just this:

*"AI, here's who you are / what you're doing / what this material is /*

*what rules to follow / what shape to output."*

*Five things. That's all.*

The rest, AI handles.

You just nitpick.

"Trim this part. Add one more example here." That's your whole job.

When you vibe code long enough, a weird moment comes.

Code keeps growing. Features keep stacking.

But the feeling that it's slipping out of your hands gets stronger.

Yesterday you thought you were building this.

Now you're just... watching from the side.

"What am I even building right now?"

The moment that question shows up, you're already one step behind.

---

What do developers spend most of their time doing?

Writing code?

Syntax?

Picking frameworks?

No.

Decisions.

Should this feature go in now, or later.

Split the structure, or just bolt it on.

Store this data now, or compute it when needed.

Developers know they're making decisions.

Because they learned that these are decisions.

Non-developers are different.

---

A non-developer starting vibe coding thinks like this:

This is just an implementation detail.

AI will handle it the smart way.

I can fix it later.

Here's the problem.

You don't even know whether the choice you're making right now is one you can never undo.

There's no criteria to tell the difference.

---

I was making these decisions constantly.

Had no idea at the time.

Whether to set up a database now or not.

Whether to build features before login.

Whether this feature actually belongs in the MVP.

Whether to save state to a file or keep it in memory.

All I had in my head was this:

"Let me just make it work first."

But looking back, what that really meant was:

"Let AI make all the decisions."

---

At first, nothing seems wrong.

Buttons click.

Screens show up.

Data appears.

But then weird things start happening.

Fix one thing. Three things break.

Nobody knows why it's built this way.

Ask AI and you get a plausible explanation.

But nobody's actually sure.

This isn't because AI is stupid.

It's because there's no record of how decisions were made, or why.

---

Developers say things like this all the time:

"This is hard to change later."

"We need to decide this now."

"We have to set the structure first."

These aren't empty words.

Developers know.

Implementation can always be changed.

But decisions, once they harden, become structure.

---

The problem with vibe coding is that the moment structure forms is completely hidden.

AI writes code.

Files appear.

Features get added one by one.

And you think:

This is still the design phase, right?

Wrong.

Structure already exists.

Decisions are already made.

Nobody told you.

---

So non-developers always fail the same way.

Fix this part.

Change the direction.

Just redo the whole thing.

By the time you're saying this on repeat, too many decisions have piled up.

AI is diligent.

It fixes everything you ask.

But all that does is paint over old decisions.

---

There's one scene that explains this perfectly.

Ever worked under a clueless boss?

Or dealt with a nightmare client?

It always starts the same way.

"Just tweak this one little thing."

So you fix it.

Then they say:

"Nice. But now just adjust this part a tiny bit."

You fix it again.

"Hmm... not there. Over here. Just a touch."

If it stopped at once, you'd be lucky.

Two, three, ten, twenty times.

"Just a tiny tweak" keeps repeating.

By that point, what you started building is gone.

The original purpose is gone.

It's not their taste.

It's not your taste.

Just some grotesque, mangled thing.

Then the boss says:

"Hmm... let's go back to the very first version.

Heh heh... that's why I keep you around!"

On those nights, that boss is what you're drinking about.

---

Why does this happen?

If you've worked under a boss who talks nonsense, you know.

Is the boss stupid?

Is the client just fickle?

No. (Well, yes!)

Decisions weren't handled in stages.

They were processed as improvised tweaks.

(No, the boss really is an idiot!)

Nobody distinguished whether this was a direction decision, a simple implementation fix, or an irreversible choice.

So every edit got layered on top of old decisions.

And the result was something nobody could take responsibility for.

In vibe coding, the exact same thing happens.

---

So there's a common piece of advice:

"Make all your decisions upfront before you start."

But the moment you throw that at a vibe coding beginner, they freeze.

What database?

What architecture?

Folder structure?

Error handling?

That's not advice.

That's paralysis.

The problem isn't that you didn't decide.

It's not that you decided too late.

It's that you tried to handle all the decisions in one big lump.

---

When I first saw a Spec Kit on GitHub, I stopped cold.

Not because the features were impressive.

Not because the tool was new.

The approach was completely different.

This isn't telling you to decide everything at once.

This is a structure that breaks decisions into bite-sized pieces.

That's when I understood.

The problem with decisions isn't volume.

It's granularity.

---

We've believed this for a while:

Give AI more context and it gets smarter.

In practice, it's the opposite.

AI is a kid with a short attention span.

When the story gets long, it zones out in the middle and nods at the end.

"Yes! Got it!"

The problem is, what it "got" isn't what you meant.

---

So a spec isn't a perfect blueprint.

A spec is closer to a meal plan -- feeding AI one bite at a time.

At this stage, decide only this much.

At this stage, execute only this much.

Nothing more. Nothing less.

---

The problem wasn't that I didn't make decisions.

Or that I tried to make them all at once.

The problem was that I couldn't control when, how much, and at what size to make them.

Without that, vibe coding keeps failing the same way.

---

Next post, I'm not going to say "make better decisions."

Instead, I'll talk about how to break decisions into smaller pieces.

Not a design you swallow whole.

A design you feed one bite at a time.

That was the first turning point where my vibe coding stopped failing.

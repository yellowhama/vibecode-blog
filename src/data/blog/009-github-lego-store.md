> **TL;DR**: An excavation of stop building. start shopping.. Real scars, no slop.

All I had was a skeleton. No -- not even that. More like a napkin sketch of what to build.

Now I needed the real stuff. Soccer match simulation. Korean name generator. School calendar. UI kit. Pixel art.

I counted on my fingers. Three days per feature. That's four months.

"When the hell am I going to finish this?"

## Just In Case

Before throwing it at Claude, I opened GitHub. No expectations. Just a hunch.

Typed "soccer simulation" into the search bar.

One second later, I felt like an idiot.

**Results: 1,823**

"...What?"

Tried again. "korean name generator"

**89.**

"godot ui kit"

**2,100.**

What had I been doing with my life?

## The open-football Gut Punch

Clicked the first result. open-football.

Started reading the README. My face got hot.

"Supports 2,147 tactical combinations."

What I built yesterday? Ten if-statements.

"Machine-learning-based player AI included."

Mine? One `random()` call.

"Real-time physics engine for ball trajectory."

Mine? Nothing. I had nothing.

Watched the demo. Players passing. Dribbling. Goalkeeper diving.

I spent two hours yesterday writing what, exactly? A number guessing game?

## MIT License. The Magic Words.

But the real shock was the license.

MIT.

Didn't know what it meant. Searched it. Rubbed my eyes.

"Use it. Sell it. Modify it. Just credit the source."

Wait. This is free?

Something this good? Just -- given away?

There's a catch, right? There's always a catch.

Read it again. No catch. Drop one LICENSE file in your repo. Done.

## Forty Minutes

Had to try it. Right then.

```bash
git clone https://github.com/openfootball/openfootball
```

Ten minutes. Downloaded.

Dropped it into my project. Fixed paths. Adjusted imports.

Twenty minutes.

Added Korean league data. K-League teams. Player names in Hangul.

Ten minutes.

Hit run.

It worked.

**Forty minutes total.**

Yesterday it took two hours to write ten if-statements. Today I integrated a finished product in forty minutes.

"How is this real?"

## The Shopping Spree

I lost my mind. In a good way.

Found korean-names-generator. 286 surnames. 100,000 first names. Trending names by decade.

What I was going to build? "Kim Cheolsu, Lee Younghee, Park Minsu." Ten names. Hardcoded.

Found school-calendar-kr. Every school in the country. Academic calendar in JSON.

What I was going to build? "March: school starts. July: summer break." Hardcoded.

Found godot-ui-pack. Buttons, sliders, popups, animations. Dark theme. Light theme.

What I was going to build? One gray rectangle.

## The Idiot's Epiphany

That's when it hit me.

I wasn't reinventing the wheel. I wasn't even close to the wheel.

I was carving a single spoke.

No -- not even a spoke. I was holding a stick and yelling "this is a wheel!"

Ten-year developers check GitHub first. Obviously.

Me? "I have to build it from scratch or it doesn't count!"

Like making noodle soup and insisting on milling the flour by hand.

## I Forgot What I Was Building

Wait. What was the goal again?

"A fun soccer management game."

Right. Fun. Not FIFA. Not a perfect simulator.

Suddenly I thought about ramen.

**"I want beef flavor in my broth!"**

Options:

A. Raise a calf. (Two-year lead time.)
B. Buy bones from a butcher. Simmer twelve hours.
C. One spoonful of beef bouillon.

The answer is C. Maybe B. Never A.

Who raises a calf to make ramen? Nobody.

But what had I been doing? Trying to write a physics engine from scratch.

And here's the kicker -- I can't even do that.

This wasn't kneading flour for noodles. I was trying to farm the wheat.

**My goal is "a fun game." Not "a perfect simulator."**

open-football is my beef bouillon.

Will players say "wow, the physics are so realistic"? No. They'll say "this is fun" and keep playing.

Nobody cares if I wrote the ML engine or pulled it from GitHub.

They care if the noodles taste good.

## Alpha in Two Days

Snapped out of it. Changed approach.

Listed every feature I needed. Searched GitHub for each one.

"[feature] MIT" -- Enter.

Over 100 stars. Recently updated. Has a README.

Clone. Drop in. Fix paths.

Two days later: alpha version. Done.

Building from scratch? A month. Two months. No -- I never would have finished. How was I going to build ML-based AI by myself?

## The Real Developer Mindset

Beginner: "I should build everything myself!"

Veteran: "Someone already built this."

Development isn't creation. It's search.

Not coding. Curation.

GitHub is a free Lego store. I'm just the person snapping pieces together.

## Failures Happened Too

Fell into a GPL trap once.

"super-soccer-engine." Perfect features. Best-in-class.

GPL licensed.

Had to open-source my own code. Commercial use? Complicated.

Trashed it.

Lesson learned: **check the license first.**

## Epilogue

Right now, 80% of my game is open source.

open-football engine. korean-names generator. godot-ui components.

The 20% I built? Connecting those pieces. Adding a Korean high school soccer club setting. Swapping the palette to pastels.

That's it.

And that's enough.

Players don't care who wrote the code. If it's fun, it's fun.
